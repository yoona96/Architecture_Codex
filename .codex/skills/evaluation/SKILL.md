# Architecture Evaluation
- Evaluation team and stakeholders are involved in this evaluation process.
- We can say that an architecture is suitable if it meets two criteria.
  1. The system that results from it will meet its quality goals. That is, the system will run predictably and fast enough to meet its performance (timing) requirements. It will be modifiable in planned ways. It will meet its security constraints. It will provide the required behavioral function. Not every quality property of a system is a direct result of its architecture, but many are, and for those that are, the architecture is suitable if it provides the blueprint for building a system that achieves those properties.
  2. The system can be built using the resources at hand: the staff, the budget, the legacy software (if any), and the time allotted before delivery. That is, the architecture is *buildable*.


## ATAM (Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method)
- ATAM is one of the architecture evaluation methods to assess quality attributes and determine which and how certain quality attribute properties should be explicitly considered in the architecture before implementing the system.
- Addressing quality attributes requires one to identify risks and non-risks associated to scenarios as well as the impact of sensitivity points on the architecture.
- The tradeoffs between competing qualities must be addessed, which often complicate the selection and prioritization of scenarios.

From Step 1 to Step 3 is to present the architecture to evaluation team and stakeholders, for the preparation of evaluation process.
In Step 1: Present the ATAM, the evaluation leader describes the evaluation method, and in Step 2: Present the business drivers, a project manager or system customer describes the business goals that would be the primary architectural drivers. In Step 3: Present the architecture, the architect describes the architecture, focusing on how it addresses the business drivers.

Since ADD 3.0 process is already done before this ATAM process and architecture document is already generated, we assume that from Step 1 to Step 3 is already done, and generate the separate document for evaluation in `/HPS/evaluation/preparation.md`. Relevant subagents should participate in generating the document. Reuse existing ADD deliverables to organize evaluation methods, project objectives, architecture baseline, participation roles, and unresolved issues, have relevant agents review them, and proceed to Step 4.

ATAM has Phase 1 and Phase 2. In Phase 1, Step 1 to 6 is proceeded, with the participants of evaluation team and project decision makers. In Phase 2, All the steps are proceeded, with the participants from Phase 1 and all stakeholders.


### Step 4: Identify the Architectural Approaches
The ATAM focuses on analyzing an architecture by understanding its architectural approaches. In this step they are captured by the evaluation team but are not analyzed. The team will ask the architect to explicitly name any identifiable approaches used, but they will also capture any approaches they heard during the architecture presentation in the previous step.

The ATAM concentrates on identifying architectural approaches and architectural styles because these represent the architecture's means of addressing the highest priority quality attributes, that is, the means of ensuring that the critical requirements are met in a predictable way.
- The architectural approaches define the important structures of the system and describe the ways in which the system can grow, respond to changes, withstand attacks, integrate with otehr systems, and so forth.
- An architectural style includes a description of component types and their topology, a description of the pattern of data and control interactions among the components, and an informal description of the benefits and drawbacks of using the style.


### Step 5: Generate the Quality Attribute Utility Tree
In this step the evaluation team works with the project decision makers (here, the architecture team, manager, and customer representatives) to identify, prioritize, and refine the system's most important quality attribute goals.

The output of the utility tree-generation step is a prioritization of specific quality attribute requirements, realized as scenarios. The utility tree serves to make concrete the quality attribute requirements, forcing the architect and customer representatives to define the relevant quality requirements precisely.

Generate utility tree following the format below.

|Quality Attribute|Refinement|Scenario|Importance|Difficulty|Sum|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|||||||

Importance and Difficulty should be in the range of 0 to 100 and in scale of 10.

### Step 6: Analyze the Architectural Approaches
In this step, the evaluation team can probe the architectural approaches that relize the important quality attributes. This is done with an eye to documenting these architectural decisions and identifying their risks, non-risks, sensitivity points, and tradeoffs.
- Sensitivity point: a decision with high leverage over one quality attribute.
- Tradeoff point: a decision that is a sensitivity point for two or more attributes that move in opposite directions.
- Risk: a decision (or absence of one) that may cause a quality attribute to go unmet.
- Non-risk: a sound, well-justified decision that needs no change.

The goal is for the evaluation team to be convinced that the instantiation of the approach in the architecture being evaluated is appropriate for meeting the attribute-specific requirements for which it is intended. Evaluation team should analyze every scenario in this step.

### Step 7: Brainstorm and Prioritize Scenarios
In this step, the evaluation team asks the stakeholders to brainstorm three kinds of scenarios: use case scenarios, growth scenarios, and exploratory scenarios.
- Use case scenarios represent the ways in which the stakeholders expect the system to be used. The stakeholder is an end user, using the system to execute some function.
- Growth scenarios represent ways in which the architecture is expected to accommodate growth and change in the moderate near term: expected modifications, changes in performance or availability, porting to other platforms, integration with other software, and so forth.
- Exploratory scenarios represent extereme forms of growth, ways in which the architecture might be stressed by changes: dramatic new performance or availability requirements, major changes in the infrastructure or mission of the system, and so forth.

Stakeholders are encouraged to consider scenarios in the utility tree that have not been analyzed; those scenarios are legitimate candidates to put into the brainstorm pool.

Once the scenarios have been collected, they must be prioritized.
1. Stakeholders are asked to merge scenarios that they believe represent the same behavior of quality concern.
2. Stakeholders vote for the scenarios they think are most important. Each stakeholder casts his or her votes publicly.
3. If two or more scenarios match and are the duplicate of one another, group them. The scenario can also go into a new leaf node of an existing branch. If the scenario fit in no branch, it might introduce risk.

The result of voting among stakeholders should be in the format below.

|ID|Scenario|Quality Attributes|Votes|
|---|---|---|---|
|||||

Stakeholders can vote to multiple scenarios up to 5 votes, which are considered to be important for their benefits. They should vote only once for one scenario. Scenarios with same score should be placed in order of numbers in ID. A scenario with higher votes has higher priority. Top 5 scenarios should be incorporated into the utility tree as new scenario.

Create or modify utility tree voting table in section 3 of `/HPS/evaluation/result.md` file.

|Quality Attribute|Refinement|Scenario|Importance|Difficulty|Sum|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|||||||

'Sum' is a sum of importance and difficulty score.

Create or modify utility tree prioritization table in section 3 of `/HPS/evaluation/result.md` file.


### Step 8: Analyze the Architectural Approaches
In this step, the architect explains how relevant architectural decisions contribute to realizing the scenario. The evaluation team performs the same activities as in Step 6. The evaluation team should also analyze every scenario in this step.

For each high-priority scenario, examine how the identified approaches satisfy (or fail) it, gathering evidence from the structure.

Modify architectural approach analysis table in section 4 of `/HPS/evaluation/result.md` file.


### Step 9: Present the results
The collected information from the ATAM needs to be summarized and presented back to the stakeholders.
The document should include:
- The architectural approaches
- The set of scenarios and their prioritization
- The utility tree
- The risks discovered
- The non-risks
- The sensitivity points and tradeoff points

Update the `/HPS/evaluation/result.md` document if necessary.
